# Hook Library

The first line is the whole game. On X, the reader decides in under two seconds whether the second line is worth it. A thread with a weak tweet 1 is dead no matter how good tweets 2–12 are. Generate hooks **first**, generate **many**, and never ship the first one.

## The rule that ranks every pattern: idea-led first

Because one page posts for many brands, the hook can't borrow credibility from *who is posting*. It has to earn attention from the **idea, the framing, and the specificity**.

- **Tier 1 — Idea-led hooks** are the default. They work with zero knowledge of who posted them.
- **Tier 2 — Identity-led hooks** (personal story, confession, personal journey) work only when a **real, ownable story** exists for that brand or subject. Never manufacture a persona, a founder, or a personal history to use them. If you don't have a true story, use Tier 1.

When in doubt, reach for Tier 1.

## Tier 1 — Idea-led hooks (default, brand-agnostic)

Each pattern: what it does · X examples · when it wins.

### Family A — Contrarian (challenge the consensus)

**A1. Bold Claim** — a strong declarative that demands agreement or argument.
- "Most 'engagement' advice is just survivorship bias with a follower count."
- "Your copy isn't too long. It's too vague."
- *Wins when:* you can back it in the body. A bold claim with no proof is noise; with tight proof it's a high-performer.

**A2. Death Declaration** — declare the old way over, name the new.
- "Single-pass prompting is dead. Make the model defend its answer."
- "The 280-character 'tip' is dead. Specificity is the new format."
- *Wins when:* the shift is real and you can show what replaces it. Template: `[Common practice] is dead. [New paradigm] is next.`

**A3. Inversion (Stop/Start)** — invert the default behavior.
- "Stop asking for answers. Start making the model argue with itself."
- "Stop chasing virality. Start building posts that compound."
- *Wins when:* the common action is genuinely counterproductive. Template: `Stop [common action]. Start [better action].`

**A4. Wrong-Approach / Bad-Advice** — name popular advice and refute it.
- "'Post every day' is bad advice. Here's what actually moves the needle."
- *Wins when:* you have the data or logic to make the refutation land, not just attitude.

### Family B — Curiosity Gap (open a loop the post closes)

Humans feel discomfort in the gap between what they know and want to know. Hint at hidden knowledge without revealing it.

**B1. Hidden Knowledge** — promise insider information.
- "What top performers won't tell you about hooks:"
- *Template:* `What [group] won't tell you about [topic]`

**B2. The Secret / The Truth** — promise a surprising reframe.
- "The uncomfortable truth about 'viral' posts: most are reverse-engineered, not lucky."
- *Templates:* `The [adjective] secret to [outcome]` · `The [surprising] truth about [topic]`

**B3. The Question** — a specific, non-obvious question that addresses the reader directly.
- "Why do most threads die on tweet 2?"
- "What if your hook isn't the problem — your second line is?"
- *Wins when:* the question is specific and non-obvious. Avoid generic questions ("Want to grow on X?"). Question hooks earn ~23% more shares than statements — but only when the question is real.

### Family C — Proof & Stakes (lead with evidence or consequence)

> Keep the lever on the **number and the finding**, not on a persona. Attribute work to the brand or state the finding impersonally ("A teardown of 500 viral threads found…"), not to an invented "I."

**C1. Statistic / Data** — open with a surprising, precise number that reframes assumptions.
- "82% of posts get near-zero reach. The other 18% share one trait."
- "A teardown of 500 viral threads: 9 of 10 open with tension, not a greeting."
- *Wins when:* the number is surprising, **specific** (precise beats round — 1,247 beats 1,000), and tied to what you're teaching.

**C2. Prediction + Stakes** — name a concept as *the* thing for a timeframe, with stakes.
- "Deliberative refinement is the 2026 writing technique that separates shipped from slop."
- *Why it works:* a dated claim creates FOMO; the category lets readers self-identify; the outcome supplies stakes. Template: `[Concept] is the [year] [category] that [outcome].`

**C3. Tribal Split** — sort readers into in-group / out-group.
- "This is what separates writers who get bookmarked from writers who get scrolled."
- *Why it works:* the reader instantly picks a side; ego drives engagement. Template: `[Technique] separates [winners] from [everyone else].`

**C4. Loss Aversion / Warning** — loss is ~2× as motivating as gain.
- "The one habit quietly killing your reach:"
- *Templates:* `Warning: [thing] is [damaging your metric]` · `The [mistake] killing your [metric]`

### Family D — Value Preview (promise a structured payoff)

**D1. List Preview** — promise scannable, discrete value.
- "7 hook patterns that print bookmarks:"
- *Wins when:* the items are genuinely useful and discrete. Numbered lists earn ~36% more engagement; 10 is the magic number, odd numbers tend to beat even.

**D2. How-To** — promise a concrete outcome with a path.
- "How to write a thread people finish (even boring topics):"
- *Templates:* `How to [achieve X] in [timeframe]` · `How to [achieve X] even if [objection]`

**D3. Before/After Compression** — show the efficiency gap (the *idea/result* version, not a personal journey).
- "What used to take a 12-step workflow now takes one prompt."
- *Why it works:* concrete gain, "I had no idea" reaction, shareable stat. Template: `What used to require [old complexity] now [new simplicity].`

### Family E — Empathy (lead with the reader's pain)

Open with the reader's struggle, not your message. Makes them feel seen.
- "If your posts get likes but no replies, the problem is your last line."
- "Writing into the void is brutal. Here's what changes it."
- *Wins when:* the pain is specific and emotionally real. Keep it about **the reader**, never about a founder's feelings.

## Tier 2 — Identity-led hooks (only with a real, ownable story)

Powerful, but they borrow from *who's speaking*. Use **only** when the brand or subject genuinely owns the story. **Never fabricate** a person, a history, or a result to unlock them.

**Story Opener** — drop the reader into a real narrative moment.
- "Last quarter a client almost fired us. The fix became our best-performing playbook."
- *Requires:* a true, specific moment. Vague stories lose readers; details create credibility.

**Confession** — lead with a genuine admission that disarms.
- "Honest admission: half our 'viral' posts were reverse-engineered, not inspired."
- *Requires:* a real confession that leads somewhere useful. Performative vulnerability backfires.

**Personal Before/After** — a true transformation with a concrete timeframe.
- "6 months ago every post flopped. One change later, replies 10×'d. Here's the change."
- *Requires:* the transformation actually happened and is the brand's to claim.

If none of these are true → return to Tier 1. A fabricated story is both a craft failure and an ethics violation.

## The plug-and-play formula bank

Fill-in skeletons for fast generation. Mix across families. (Tier-1/idea-led unless noted.)

**Curiosity-gap**
- `What [group] won't tell you about [topic]`
- `The [adjective] secret to [outcome]`
- `[Number] things most [people] never figure out about [topic]`
- `[Result] — but not the way you think`

**Contrarian**
- `[Common practice] is dead. [New paradigm] is next.`
- `Stop [common action]. Start [better action].`
- `[Popular opinion] is wrong — here's why`
- `Why [successful group] don't [common practice]`

**Listicle**
- `[Number] ways to [achieve X] without [sacrifice]`
- `[Number] [topic] mistakes destroying your [metric]`
- `[Number] things I wish I knew before [X]` *(Tier 2 if first-person)*
- `[Number] [topic] tips that [impressive result]`

**How-to**
- `How to [achieve X] in [timeframe] (step by step)`
- `How to [achieve X] even if [common objection]`
- `How [authority/brand] [achieved X] — broken down`

**Question**
- `Is [common belief] actually [myth/true]?`
- `What happens when you [action] for [time]?`
- `Can [ordinary thing] really [impressive claim]?`

**Prediction / Authority**
- `[Concept] is the [year] [category] that [outcome]`
- `[Number] [topic] shifts coming in [year] you can't ignore`

**Problem / Solution**
- `[Common problem]? Here's the fix.`
- `The real reason [problem] keeps happening`
- `Why your [thing] isn't working (and what to do)`

**Negative / Loss aversion**
- `[Number] [topic] mistakes destroying your [metric]`
- `Warning: [thing] is [damaging your thing]`
- `The hidden danger of [common practice]`

**Comparison**
- `[A] vs [B]: which actually [outcome]?`
- `We tried [A] and [B] — here's the winner`

## The hook generation process

When given a topic:

1. **Generate 10–20 hooks** across different families above. Don't settle for 5 — the Upworthy method was 25 variations per headline for a reason. Volume surfaces the outlier.
2. **Label each** with its pattern name (so the user learns the system).
3. **Score** each on the matrix below.
4. **Shortlist the top 2–3** (7+ score) and mark one recommended pick with one sentence of reasoning.
5. Hand the winners to the body-building step in `x-playbook.md`.

**Output shape:**

```
--- Hooks for: [topic] ---
1. [Pattern]: [hook]
2. [Pattern]: [hook]
...
★ Recommended: #[n] — [one sentence: why this one].
```

## Hook scoring (target 7+)

| Criteria | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|----------|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity** | no gap | mild interest | strong pull | must-click |
| **Specificity** | generic | topic-specific | has numbers | hyper-specific |
| **Emotion** | neutral | mild trigger | clear emotion | high-arousal |

Sum the three. **Ship 7+.** Below 7, regenerate — usually the fix is more specificity or a sharper emotional angle.

## X-specific hook rules

- **One line.** The hook tweet should land its tension in the first sentence, ideally under ~200 characters (the whole tweet under 280).
- **Curiosity or tension up front** — not context, not a greeting, not a windup.
- **No "1/" numbering** on thread openers — it reads as "here comes a lecture" and underperforms. Use a clean standalone line; an optional `🧵` or `↓` can signal "thread," but the line must work without it.
- **Contrarian and bold-claim hooks** pull the most replies and quote-tweets on X; **list and how-to** hooks pull the most bookmarks. Pick by the goal of the post.
- **Every word earns its place.** If a word can be cut without losing meaning, cut it.

## Research-backed hook rules (the numbers)

- **Optimal headline length:** ~11 words / 65 characters (BuzzSumo, 100M headlines). Specificity beats brevity; too short underperforms.
- **Negative superlatives +63% CTR** vs positive (Outbrain). "The worst…" / "stop…" / "killing your…" frames outperform "the best…".
- **Question headlines:** +23% share rate over statements (when specific).
- **List headlines with numbers:** +36% engagement. **10** is the magic number; **odd > even**.
- **Specific beats round:** 1,247 > 1,000; "in 48 hours" > "fast."
- **High-arousal emotions drive sharing** (Berger & Milkman): awe +30%, practical value +34%, surprise +25%. Low-arousal (contentment, mild sadness) does not spread. Anger/anxiety/outrage spread too — use the ethical ones.

## Hook anti-patterns (cut on sight)

- ❌ "You won't believe…" / "This is huge" — dated clickbait with no substance.
- ❌ Generic questions anyone could ask ("Want to grow on X?").
- ❌ "I'm excited to share…" / "I'm proud to announce…" — AI/announcement tell (and identity-led).
- ❌ ALL CAPS, `!!!`, emoji walls — read as spam, suppress trust.
- ❌ A hook that promises what the body can't pay off — the fastest way to lose the reader on tweet 2.
- ❌ Identity bait with no real story behind it.
