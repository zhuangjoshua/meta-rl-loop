# AI Tells & Humanization

Nothing kills a post's credibility faster than reading like a machine wrote it. This is the hunt-and-replace catalog plus the humanization passes. Run it on **every** draft before shipping.

**The one test that catches most of it:** read the post aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a smart friend at a bar, rewrite it.

---

## The tell catalog

### Transition tells (delete the runway, start with substance)
| ❌ AI tell | ✅ Fix |
|-----------|--------|
| "Here's the thing:" | [remove entirely] |
| "Here's the wild part:" | [just state it] |
| "Here's what's interesting:" | [let it be interesting] |
| "But here's the kicker:" | [direct statement] |
| "Let's dive in" / "Let me explain" | [start explaining] |
| "The key takeaway is:" | [integrate into the line] |
| "Buckle up" / "First things first" | [never] |

### Enthusiasm tells (if you have to say it's exciting, it isn't)
Delete: "I'm excited to share," "game-changer," "revolutionary," "groundbreaking," "transformative," "mind-blowing," "incredible," "amazing," "powerful" (of ideas), "leveraging the power of." Let the content create the excitement.

### Structure tells (prose flows; structure serves meaning)
Avoid: "First… Second… Third… Finally…," numbered lists for everything, "In conclusion," "To summarize," "Let's break this down," headers on every line, excessive bullets.

### Engagement bait (the algorithm and the reader both discount it)
Cut: "Change my mind," "What do you think?," "Let me know in the comments," "RT if you agree," "Drop a 🔥," "Tag someone who needs this," "Agree or disagree?" End on a command or a quotable line instead (see `x-playbook.md` closers).

### Corporate speak
| ❌ | ✅ |
|----|----|
| leverage / utilize | use |
| implement | build, do, try |
| facilitate | help |
| optimize | improve |
| streamline | simplify |
| robust | strong, solid |
| ecosystem | system, tools |
| synergy | [delete] |
| best practices | what works |
| deep dive | look closely |
| moving forward | next |

### Hedging (over-balancing reads as having no point)
Trim: "It's worth noting that…," "That being said…," "On the other hand…," "Your mileage may vary," "Of course, there are exceptions," "To be fair…." Hedge **only** when the nuance genuinely changes the claim.

### Filler (if removing it loses nothing, remove it)
Cut: "It goes without saying," "Needless to say," "As we all know," "fundamentally," "essentially," "basically," "in essence," "when it comes to," "in terms of," "at the end of the day."

### Meta-commentary (do the thing; don't announce it)
Cut: "In this thread, I'll…," "Let me walk you through…," "Here's a breakdown of…," "I want to share…." Just deliver.

### The "sounds smart" crutch
Use only when it genuinely fits, never as filler: "paradigm shift," "mental model," "first principles," "unlock," "supercharge," "10x," "level up."

### Emoji overuse
Emojis are seasoning, not the dish. 0–2 per post on X; never as bullet points, never starting every line, no 🚀💡🔥 walls.

## Punctuation antipatterns (2024–2025 detection signals)

- **Em-dash overuse** — like this — everywhere. Cap at ~1 per 500 words; use commas or restructure.
- **Paragraph/line starters + comma:** "However," "Moreover," "Overall," "Furthermore," "Importantly," — integrate the contrast mid-sentence or delete.
- **Pleonasms:** "true fact" → fact · "end result" → result · "completely eliminate" → eliminate · "close proximity" → nearby.
- **Tautologies:** "collaborate together" → collaborate · "revert back" → revert · "repeat again" → repeat.
- **Uniform sentence length (low burstiness).** AI writes everything 15–18 words. Humans don't. Add fragments. Vary hard. "Not every line needs a verb. Sometimes a phrase hits harder."
- **Always-on Oxford comma / Latin-root words.** Occasional variation is human; prefer "use" over "utilize," "show" over "demonstrate," "about" over "approximately." Keep multi-syllable words under ~20% of the vocabulary.

## Claudeisms — the structural tells

Vocabulary tells are easy. These are the *rhythm and structure* habits that make even a "humanized" draft still read as a language model. They survive a normal edit, so hunt them on purpose.

**1. The antithesis-negation flip — "It's not X, it's Y." / "You didn't X. You Y'd it."**
The single most common LLM move. One is a punch; three is a fingerprint.
- Tell: *"You didn't acquire demand. You refunded it."*
- Fix: say it once, plainly, and rough up the symmetry — or drop the mirror. **Max one per post.**

**2. The rule of three — "Same X. Same Y. Same Z."**
Three balanced items is the default model cadence.
- Fix: use two, or four where the last one breaks the pattern, or one strong item. Never three clean parallels in a row.

**3. The fortune-cookie closer.**
Ending on a tidy paradox or aphorism: *"Sounding polished is how you go invisible." "The price is the price."*
- Fix: end on a concrete detail, a specific image, or a slightly anticlimactic real thought. People rarely land a perfect aphorism — they trail off or get specific.

**4. Mirror symmetry / chiasmus — "Morning brain builds. Afternoon brain reacts."**
- Fix: break the balance; let one half run long and messy.

**5. Even weighting.**
Every sentence the same polished weight, each doing one clean job. Real writing is lumpy.
- Fix: one run-on, one fragment, a parenthetical, an "anyway." Let it be uneven.

**6. Colon setups — "The expensive part:" "Here's the kicker:" "The truth:"**
- Fix: delete the setup, say the thing.

**7. Filler intensifiers — "just," "quietly," "simply," "genuinely," "actually," "literally."**
Sprinkled for rhythm and faux-ominous mood ("*quietly* becomes the real price").
- Fix: delete on sight unless load-bearing. Cap "just" at one per post.

**8. The reflexive hedge-pivot — "This isn't X. But…" / "None of this means Y."**
- Fix: keep only if the caveat is real and short; otherwise it's throat-clearing.

**9. Grand naming — "the uncomfortable truth about…," Title-Cased Concepts, "the real reason…"**
- Fix: drop the fanfare; lead with the thing itself.

**Quick self-scan:** count the "not X, it's Y" flips, the threes, and the "just"s; check the last line isn't a fortune cookie. If any single line could headline a LinkedIn broetry post, rewrite it.

## The humanization passes

After structure is solid, run these in order:

1. **Read-aloud / bar test.** Would you say this to a friend? Rewrite anything that fails.
2. **Transition audit.** Delete every "Here's the thing"–type phrase.
3. **Enthusiasm check.** Cut the excitement adjectives; keep the exciting content.
4. **Specificity check.** Replace every generic noun with a concrete example or precise number. (≥1 concrete example per abstract claim.)
5. **Length check.** Cut ~20%. Viral copy is almost always shorter than the draft.
6. **De-Claude pass.** Run the structural scan above: count the "not X, it's Y" flips, the threes, the "just"/"quietly" filler; confirm the last line isn't a fortune cookie. Break any structure that repeats. Aim for lumpy, specific, and a little anticlimactic.

**What to preserve while humanizing** (don't sand these off): the hook's tension, the bold claim, the tribal/contrarian framing, and the quotable closer. Humanize the *delivery*, not the *spine*.

## Voice calibration (brand-neutral by default)

Because the page posts for **many brands**, there is no fixed personal voice to match — and **no founder persona to borrow**. Default to a clean, brand-neutral craft voice and bend it to the *topic and brand register*, never to an invented individual.

**Default X craft voice:**
- **Punchy.** Short sentences. Fragments allowed. White space is your friend.
- **Specific.** Concrete nouns, precise numbers, real examples.
- **Confident but evidenced.** Strong conviction + specific proof.
  - Overconfident with no proof → gets dunked on in replies.
  - Underconfident / hedged → doesn't spread.
  - Target: a strong claim you immediately back up.
- **Human, not corporate.** Plain verbs. No jargon. Contractions are fine.

**Register dial (match to the brand/topic, not a persona):** a fintech teardown reads more precise and sober; a consumer-culture take reads more playful and irreverent. Same craft rules, different temperature. Decide the register from the *subject*, and keep it consistent within a post.

**Do not** write in a fixed first-person "founder" voice, invent a backstory, or imply the post is a personal confession unless a real, ownable story was supplied (see `hook-library.md` Tier 2).

## Worked example: AI draft → human viral

**Before (every tell firing):**
> I'm excited to share a revolutionary productivity hack that will change your workflow forever. Here's the thing: most people waste hours on email. Let's dive into how inbox zero can transform your day. First, you batch process. Second, you use templates. Finally, you schedule check-ins. What do you think?

**After (human, specific, X-ready):**
> Email before noon is self-sabotage.
>
> Tested it for 3 weeks. No inbox until 2pm. Deep-work hours went from 2 to 4+.
>
> That 7:47am Slack ping isn't your fire. Morning brain builds. Afternoon brain reacts.
>
> Flip the order or you're always on defense.

**What changed:** removed enthusiasm tells ("excited," "revolutionary") and transition tells ("Here's the thing," "Let's dive in"); killed the rigid "First/Second/Finally"; dropped the engagement-bait closer ("What do you think?"); added concrete specifics (3 weeks, 2→4+ hours, 7:47am); varied sentence length from 4 words to 15; opened on conviction instead of an announcement; ended on a command, not a question.

## Final checklist (before shipping)

- [ ] No transition tells, especially in the first line.
- [ ] No enthusiasm adjectives; no corporate verbs.
- [ ] No engagement-bait closer; ends on a command or quotable.
- [ ] No meta-commentary, no "1/" numbering, no emoji wall.
- [ ] Sentence length varies; at least one fragment.
- [ ] Every abstract claim has a concrete example or number.
- [ ] No invented persona, history, or first-person founder voice.
- [ ] Passes the read-aloud bar test — wouldn't embarrass you said out loud.
