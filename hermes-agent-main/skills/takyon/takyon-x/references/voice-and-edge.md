# Voice & Edge

The gap between "technically correct" and "I have to reply to this" is **conviction + emotion + a little edge.** Structure (hooks in `hook-library.md`, construction in `x-playbook.md`) gets you a clean post. This file is the *voice* layer that makes it spread — and the guardrail that keeps it from tipping into cheap ragebait.

## The one principle: edge is seasoning, not the meal

- **Slightly unhinged = ~80% real point, ~20% attitude.** The insight carries the post; the edge makes people feel something about it.
- **Delete-the-edge test:** strip the attitude. If there's still a true, useful, or funny idea underneath — keep the edge. If nothing's left, it was ragebait. Cut it.
- A spicy *true* take spreads and earns trust. A spicy *lie* is a liability that ages into an apology.

## Why edge works (psychology, used ethically)

- **High-arousal emotions spread; low-arousal ones don't.** Anger actually drives shares *more* than joy or sadness (Beihang study); awe, surprise, and humor are also high-arousal. The ethical lever is **righteous frustration at a bad *practice*, not manufactured outrage at *people*.**
- **Assertions out-engage questions.** A flat, confident claim makes people pile in to agree or argue (the engagement reading of *Cunningham's Law*). State your take; don't ask permission. *(Caveat: this means a strong **opinion** stated confidently — never a deliberately false **fact** posted to bait "well, actually" corrections. That's the banned version — see guardrails.)*
- **First 30–60 minutes of replies/reposts is the single strongest ranking signal.** Debatable, opinionated takes earn fast replies; that velocity is what the algorithm rewards (see `x-playbook.md`).
- **91% of people favor brands that use humor.** Edge + funny beats edge + mean every time.

## The engagement-voice moves

1. **Conviction, zero hedging.** Cut "I think / maybe / kind of / arguably." Say it flat, then back it. Loud + evidenced, never just loud.
2. **Say the quiet part out loud.** Name the thing your audience secretly believes but won't post. Instant "finally, someone said it."
   - *"Half of 'data-driven' teams are just decorating a decision they already made."*
3. **Pick a villain — a practice, never a person.** Give readers something to be against. Punch **up** (incumbents, lazy norms, tired advice); never **down**.
   - *"Your 40-page brand guidelines are a coffin for anything anyone would actually share."*
4. **Twist the knife.** Don't just name the problem — let them feel it for one beat before the turn.
5. **Oddly-specific, slightly absurd images.** Hyperbole and weirdly precise details read as human and funny.
   - *"Every B2B homepage is the same purple gradient and one sentence no human has said out loud."*
6. **Deadpan and self-aware.** The "unhinged" texture is confidence + dryness + willingness to be a little weird. Commit to the bit. **Strategic** weirdness that fits the brand — not random chaos for its own sake.
7. **Roast the category, the cliché, or yourself — with affection.** Ryanair/Wendy's energy. Mock the thing, not the customer's worth as a person.
8. **End on the spiciest line, not the safest.** The screenshot line should be the one with the most attitude that's still true.

## The "unhinged" dial (default low)

A 0–10 attitude dial. **Default 3–4 — that's "slightly."**

| Dial | Voice | Fits |
|------|-------|------|
| 0–2 | Buttoned-up, dry wit at most | finance, healthcare, enterprise, sensitive topics |
| **3–4** | **Confident, opinionated, the occasional absurd specific** ← default | most brands |
| 5–7 | Openly irreverent, roasts the category, memey | challenger/consumer brands |
| 8–10 | Full chaos (Duolingo/Liquid Death) | rare — only audiences who already expect it |

Set the dial from the **brand and topic**, keep it consistent within a single post, and when unsure, sit at 3–4. For a multi-brand page this is a per-brand decision — a line that fits Liquid Death will sink a wealth manager.

## Worked example: mild → too far

Take: *discounting erodes pricing power.*

- **No edge (dial 0):** "Frequent discounts can weaken pricing power over time." → true, forgettable.
- **Slightly unhinged (dial 4, ship this):** "Your 'best quarter ever' was just you bribing loyal customers to wait for the next sale. You played yourself." → keeps the insight, adds attitude and a villain (the practice).
- **Too far (ragebait, cut it):** "If you run discounts you're an idiot who deserves to go bankrupt." → insult, punches at people, zero added insight. Fails the delete-the-edge test.

## Guardrails — edgy, not ragebait

| ✅ Spicy (do) | ❌ Ragebait (never) |
|--------------|---------------------|
| A strong, **true** opinion | Skewed, cherry-picked, or false "facts" |
| Punch **up** at practices/incumbents | Punch **down** at people, identity, protected groups |
| Righteous frustration at a bad norm | Manufactured outrage / fake victimhood |
| A confident claim that invites debate | A deliberately wrong *fact* to farm corrections |
| Roast the category, the cliché, or yourself | Roast your own customers as people |
| Edge that amplifies a real point | Edge with no point (engagement farming) |

**Hard nos** (reinforced in `SKILL.md` Rules): politics, identity, or tragedy used as bait; anything that would need an apology tomorrow; lies for outrage; dunking on the vulnerable. *If a joke needs a disclaimer, it wasn't worth the likes.*

## Brand-fit & when to skip

- **Not every brand should be edgy.** Forced edge reads as try-hard and burns credibility. Match the dial to the brand; test small before turning it up.
- **Best carriers:** Contrarian, Tribal-split, and Loss-aversion hooks from `hook-library.md` — they're built to hold a strong opinion.
- **Skip the edge for:** pure how-to / reference threads (bookmark-bait wants clarity, not heat), genuinely sensitive subjects, and any crisis/PR moment. Heat and "save-worthy" are different jobs.
