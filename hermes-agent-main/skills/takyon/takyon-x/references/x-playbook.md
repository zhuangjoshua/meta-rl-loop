# X Playbook

How to turn a winning hook into a finished post or thread, and which **content-level** levers earn reach. Everything here is about the copy and its structure — not the account. (For hooks, see `hook-library.md`. For removing AI voice, see `ai-tells.md`.)

---

## Tweet anatomy: hook → body → closer

Every post, single or thread, has three jobs.

### 1. Hook (first 2 seconds)
Stop the scroll and open a loop. Built in `hook-library.md`. The body and closer exist to pay it off.

### 2. Body: the build (in this order)
1. **WHAT** — the idea in 1–2 sentences. Say the thing before you explain why it matters.
2. **HOW** — the mechanic, with a concrete example. This is where specificity lives.
3. **WHY NOW** — what changed that makes this possible/true/urgent.
4. **PAYOFF** — what the reader can now do, build, or avoid.

**Anti-patterns:** selling "why it matters" before stating "what it is"; generic benefits with no mechanic; abstractions with no example.

### 3. Closer: command, not request
End on authority, not a beg.

- ❌ "Try it. What do you think?" (engagement bait — and the algorithm has learned to discount it)
- ✅ "Your next post shouldn't open with a greeting. It should open with the tension." (mirrors the thesis, gives a next step, ends strong)

A great closer is **screenshot-bait**: a line so tight people quote it on its own. Write the closer to be liftable out of context.

## Single (atomic) tweets

One sharp idea, fully self-contained. The workhorse format.

**Shapes that work:**
- **Observation:** "[Thing] is underrated. Here's the part people miss: …"
- **Listicle (micro):** "4 ways to make a thread people finish:\n\n1. …"
- **Contrarian:** "Unpopular opinion: [claim the body defends]."
- **Lesson:** "Spent [time] on [thing]. The one lesson: [insight]."
- **Framework:** "[Concept] in 30 seconds: …"

**Rules:**
- **Under ~200 characters** tends to outperform — tighter is punchier.
- **One idea per tweet.** If it has two ideas, it's two tweets (or a thread).
- **No link in the body.** Put any link in a reply (see algorithm levers).
- **Questions drive replies**, and replies are the strongest reach signal — end an atomic tweet on a genuine question when the goal is discussion (a real question, not "thoughts??").

## Thread engineering

Use a thread for a sequence, an argument, a teardown, or a step-by-step. Not for one idea stretched thin.

**Structure:**
- **Tweet 1 — Hook.** Standalone viral potential. Stops the scroll in the first line. No "1/".
- **Tweet 2 — Promise / setup.** Name the problem or state what the reader will walk away with.
- **Tweets 3…N-1 — One idea per tweet.** Each is bookmark-worthy on its own and earns the next. 3–4 lines max; never a wall of text.
- **Final tweet — Payoff + quotable closer.** Resolve the loop and leave a screenshot line.

**Rules:**
- **5–12 tweets** is the sweet spot. Under 5 feels thin; over 12 loses people.
- **Each tweet must stand alone** — assume any single tweet is the only one a reader sees (quote-tweeters lift the middle).
- **Line breaks for readability**; one idea per tweet.
- **Consistency is everything** — one weak tweet tanks the whole thread's perceived quality. Cut the weakest tweet rather than carry it.
- **No "1/ 2/ 3/" numbering in the posted text.** It signals "lecture incoming" and underperforms. If you must cue continuation, a single `🧵` or `↓` on tweet 1 is enough.
- **CTA, brand-agnostic.** End on the idea or a soft engagement nudge ("Bookmark this for your next thread"). Do **not** end with "follow [account] for more" — the account isn't the brand and following it isn't the goal. The win is the post spreading, getting bookmarked, and getting quoted.

**Thread skeleton (fill in):**

```
[1] [Hook — bold claim or curiosity gap. Standalone. No "1/".]

[2] The problem:
    [what most people get wrong, 2–3 lines]

[3] The fix:
    [core mechanic in 2–3 lines + one concrete example]

[4] How it works:
    [step] → [step] → [step]
    [why each step matters]

[5] What changes:
    • [concrete outcome 1]
    • [concrete outcome 2]
    • [concrete outcome 3]

[6] [Payoff restated as a command + a quotable one-liner to screenshot.]

[reply] [any link goes here, never in the tweets above]
```

## Quote tweets (standalone authority)

A QT that adds genuine value can spread on its own, independent of who posts it.

- **Formula:** original tweet **+ your distinct take.**
- Add data the original missed, supply a counterpoint or the missing nuance, or compress it into a sharper frame.
- **Never** post "This." / "So true." / "100%." — those add nothing and earn nothing.
- The QT line must work as a standalone tweet — because in feeds, it often appears as one.

## Replies (standalone reach)

A reply is content. A sharp reply on a large thread can out-reach the original.

- **Make it tweet-worthy** — write it as if it were its own post.
- **Add value or a real angle**, not "great post!" Insightful or genuinely contrarian replies get quote-tweeted, which is free reach.
- The reach here comes from the **quality of the reply**, not from the size of the account being replied to. Don't frame reply work as a follower-growth tactic — frame it as "this reply could go viral by itself."

**Read the situation, then pick one move.** A reply is one move in a live exchange, not a mini-essay. The sharpest ones **answer the thing underneath** — the smug premise, the false tradeoff, the outdated assumption, the gatekeeping — instead of the literal claim.

| When the take is… | Move | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Inflated, not worth a real fight | **Deadpan deflation** | Undercut it; don't argue. *"Impressive amount of conviction for a guess."* |
| Beaten by a real product or option contrast | **Concrete jab** | Name the actual difference. *"That fixes the demo. The hard part was everything after it."* |
| Killed by a number or a constraint | **Numeric collapse** | Drop the count and leave. *"Four steps to do what should take zero. That's the review."* |
| One a direct rebuttal would sound robotic against | **Sideways analogy** | Reframe it. *"People said that about online banking too."* |
| Flatly wrong | **Blunt correction** | One line, no cleanup. *"You're describing a habit and calling it a principle."* |
| Just performing certainty | **Bored dismissal** | Low energy, used sparingly. *"Bold take for 2019."* |

**Reply discipline:** one move per reply, usually 1–2 sentences, no cleanup paragraph. End a beat early and leave something implied. Set **heat** with the edge dial (`voice-and-edge.md`) — calm for good-faith disagreement, sharper for smug takes, combative only if they attacked first. Keep it from reading like a bot with the de-Claude pass (`ai-tells.md`) — above all, don't reflexively reach for the "X isn't Y, it's Z" rebuttal.

## TL;DR formulas (for thread/teardown closers or recap tweets)

- **Definition + compression + outcome:** `[Concept] = [plain-language definition]. [Before/after]. [Result].`
- **Command + mechanic + stakes:** `[Do this] instead of [that]. [How]. [Why it matters].`
- **Comparison + implication:** `[Old way] optimizes for [weak outcome]. [New way] optimizes for [strong outcome].`

A good TL;DR is quotable enough to go viral detached from the thread.

## Content-level X algorithm levers

What the platform rewards and punishes **about the copy itself**. (Posting cadence, follower count, and timing are profile/operational levers — out of scope here.)

**What earns reach:**

| Signal | Weight | How the copy earns it |
|--------|--------|----------------------|
| **Replies received** | Very high | Reply-worthy content: real questions, debates, takes people need to respond to. |
| **Time spent reading (dwell)** | High | Threads and longer single tweets with line breaks; a hook that makes people read to the end. |
| **Bookmarks** | High | Tactical, save-worthy content — lists, frameworks, step-by-steps, reference-grade tweets. |
| **Reposts / quotes** | Medium | Shareable insight or a bold, defensible take; a quotable closer. |
| **Likes** | Low–medium | Easy agreement, relatable lines. Nice, not decisive. |

**What kills reach (fix before shipping):**

- **Links in the tweet body.** Move every link to the first reply.
- **More than ~2 hashtags.** Read as spam; 0–1 is plenty, often zero.
- **Engagement-bait phrasing** ("RT if you agree," "comment X below") — discounted by the algorithm and reads as AI.
- **A wall of text** with no line breaks — tanks dwell time.
- **An inconsistent thread** — one weak tweet drags the whole thing down.
- **Editing the post within the first ~30 minutes** of posting (disrupts early distribution).

**Practical implication:** decide the post's job before writing the closer. Want **replies** → end on a genuine question or a debatable claim. Want **bookmarks** → make it a tight, numbered, reference-grade payoff. Want **quotes/reposts** → land a screenshot-worthy one-liner.

## Media (light note)

Native image/video can lift dwell time and stop the scroll, but this skill ships **copy**. If suggesting media, keep it minimal and in service of the idea (one clear visual, legible on mobile). Never make the copy depend on the media to make sense — the words must carry the post alone.

## Platform length cheat-sheet (X)

| Element | Target | Hard limit |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Single tweet | < 200 chars | 280 chars |
| Thread hook (tweet 1) | one line, < 200 chars | 280 chars |
| Per thread tweet | 3–4 lines | 280 chars |
| Thread length | 5–12 tweets | — |

When a draft exceeds these, cut — don't cram. Tighter almost always wins on X.
