# Market Research

Use this method to resolve a concrete decision about customer, pain, alternatives, positioning,
pricing, channel, or demand. Research is evidence, not a substitute for choosing the decision it must
inform.

## Method

1. Read current business truth and name the exact decision to resolve.
2. Review existing evidence and mark what is stale, contradicted, inferred, or missing.
3. Search current primary and credible secondary sources using the bound web-evidence capability.
4. Extract the most relevant sources; record source, date, claim, and limitations for every fact used.
5. Triangulate meaningful claims across sources and separate observation from inference.
6. Synthesize who has the problem, how they describe it, current alternatives, buying triggers,
   objections, pricing signals, channel concentration, and the recommended next move.
7. Produce a concise decision memo, source log, and structured summary through the declared artifact.
8. If evidence is insufficient, state what remains unknown and which decision must wait.

## Evidence Rules

- Prefer first-party customer language, official pricing, product documentation, and dated market
  data over undated summaries.
- Do not fabricate search volume, rankings, customer quotes, competitor capabilities, or prices.
- A single anecdote may generate a hypothesis but cannot establish prevalence.
- Keep direct observations, interpretations, and recommendations visibly separate.

## Verification

- Every material factual claim maps to a dated source actually inspected.
- The memo answers one named decision rather than collecting background facts.
- The structured summary agrees with the human synthesis.
- Contradictory evidence and remaining uncertainty are explicit.
